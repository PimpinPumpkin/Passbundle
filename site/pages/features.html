Autofill, passkeys, sharing, two-step login, and reports are all here. This page is about what we added that other password managers don't have, from filling the odd field on your bank's sign-in page to setting up a whole office with one command.

Fill picker

You decide what goes in each field.

Right-click a field, or use the button in PassBundle's menu inside it, and choose what fills it: the username, the password, the one-time code, any custom field, or nothing at all. PassBundle remembers the choice on that item and fills it there every time, on every device.

  • A custom field fills a field with a different name, no HTML reading
  • Each item lists its choices in plain words, and you can remove one
  • A choice only ever fills on the site it was made on

Rollout

One command sets up every computer, browsers included.

Run the install command, with our install script, from your RMM, Intune, or group policy. It turns off the browsers' own password saving, and once the extension is in the Chrome and Edge stores, it installs it with your server filled in and puts each person in the right organization when they sign up.

  • Turns saving off without deleting anything, so people can import first
  • Can turn off the browsers' card and address autofill too
  • Limit who can join with it to your email domains

Import from your browser

Bring your browser's passwords without the mess.

PassBundle walks you through your browser's own export, step by step for Chrome, Edge, Brave, Firefox, and Safari, then sorts it: what you already have is left out, and a login whose password differs from yours goes to a folder to check, not over the top of it.

  • Duplicates in the file are left out too
  • A reminder to delete the export, with how on your computer
  • Then how to turn the browser's own saving off

Script access

Credentials for your RMM scripts, without pasting them in.

Give a script a token that reads only the items you choose, then call Get-PassBundleCredential from PowerShell. The script never holds more than it needs, and you see every read.

  • Tokens expire, can be limited to your networks, and stop the moment you revoke them
  • End-to-end encrypted: the server can't read what a token reads
  • Nothing to install, on Windows PowerShell 5.1 or PowerShell 7. It isn't code-signed yet, so computers that run only signed scripts refuse it

Also only in PassBundle

  • No confirm stepPeople you invite, and people who join with the install command, get in without anyone clicking Confirm, with a check a hacked server can't fake.
  • Default policies, and required onesAn MSP sets policies once for every client. A client can set its own, unless you make one required for every client.
  • Site fixesFix a site with the fill picker and, if you like, share the fix. Once three people make the same one, it fills for everyone on the server; MSPs can apply their staff's fixes to every client.
  • Folders down to the capabilityLet a team see a folder's items without their passwords, or add to a folder without changing it.
  • Item historyEvery edit keeps the version before, with who saved it, and you can restore it.
  • Templates and expiration datesWi-Fi, servers, licenses, certificates, and your own, with reminders from the browser extension before something expires.
  • One-time codes stay codesA code typed into a sign-in page is never saved as your password.
  • Events to your security toolsMicrosoft Sentinel, syslog over TLS, or a signed webhook, from one client or every client at once. Not yet tried with Sentinel, Huntress, or Blumira themselves.
  • Private by default on the desktopOn Windows and Mac, the desktop app keeps itself out of screenshots and screen sharing unless you allow them.
