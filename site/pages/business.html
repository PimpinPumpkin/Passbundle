Set up from the start

Account recovery, strong master passwords, one work account per person, and shared collections for everyone and for admins are on before anyone joins. Change any of them in one place.

No confirm step

Invite someone, or let them join from the extension your rollout installed, and they're in. Your admins' apps finish the job in the background, and the owners get an email saying who joined.

Forgotten master passwords

With account recovery, an admin can reset a member's master password and the vault comes back intact. Everyone is told about it when they join.

Rollout

Every computer, in an afternoon

One PowerShell command sets up a Windows computer. Run it from Intune, group policy, or any RMM.

  • Installs the extension in Chrome and Edge, with your server already filled in
  • Turns off the browsers' own password managers, without deleting what's in them
  • Anyone who then signs in joins your organization
  • Limit it to your email domains, so only your own people can use it
  • Built for rollout day: a whole office behind one internet connection can sign up at once
Policies

Rules that explain themselves

Require two-step login, set master password strength, keep work accounts separate from personal ones, and decide whether people can export vaults. Every policy says in a sentence what it does.

  • Account recovery with automatic enrollment, on by default
  • Master passwords of at least 12 characters, checked for strength
  • Custom roles, for a manager who handles people but not policies

Everything else you'd expect

Templates for your team

Share item templates for the things your company keeps: vendor portals, servers, licenses. Everyone starts from the same fields.

Reports and event logs

See weak, reused, and exposed passwords in shared collections, and who viewed or changed what.

Expiring credentials

Certificates, API keys, and domains carry their expiration date, and the people who use them are reminded before it passes.

End-to-end encrypted

Shared items are encrypted with your organization's key, which only your members' devices hold. We never have a key that can open your company's vault.

Work and personal stay apart

A work account belongs to one organization, so people keep their own passwords in a separate personal account. When someone leaves, shared passwords stay with the company.

Run it yourself if you like

PassBundle is open source. Use our hosted service, or run the server on your own hardware.

Does it work on Macs?

Yes. The extension and the web vault work on Mac, Windows, and Linux. The rollout command and Windows Hello unlock are for Windows today; Mac rollout and Touch ID are next.

Is there an app for phones?

Not yet. iPhone and Android apps are coming. Until then, people can open the web vault in their phone's browser.

Do you support single sign-on or directory sync?

Not yet. People sign in with their own master password, and you add them by email, with the rollout command, or from the admin console.

Can we move from another password manager?

Yes. Import a shared export into your organization's collections, and each person brings their own passwords. See Switch to PassBundle.
