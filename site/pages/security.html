Security

A password manager has to earn trust. Here's how PassBundle is built, what we can and can't see, and how to tell us if you find a problem.

Your vault is encrypted on your device

  • Your master password never leaves your device. PassBundle turns it into a key with PBKDF2-SHA256 at 600,000 rounds, or Argon2id if you choose it, salted with your email address.
  • Everything in your vault is encrypted with AES-256, with an HMAC-SHA256 check on every item, before it's saved or synced. That includes names, usernames, website addresses, notes, and files, not just passwords.
  • Sharing keeps the encryption. A shared vault has its own key, which travels to each member encrypted to that member's own RSA key. The server passes it along but can't open it.
  • Sends are encrypted with a key that's only in the link you share, never on our servers.
  • Checking for breaches doesn't reveal your passwords. To see whether a password is in a known breach, the app sends only the first five characters of its SHA-1 hash to Have I Been Pwned, and compares the answer on your device.

The server can't decrypt

Our servers store your encrypted vault and a hash that proves it's you when you sign in. That hash is hashed again on the server before it's stored, and it can't be used to decrypt anything. PassBundle's server never holds a key that can open a vault. Any feature that would need one gets redesigned or dropped.

That also means we can't recover your vault if you forget your master password. Businesses can turn on account recovery, which lets their own admins reset a member's master password; see our privacy policy for exactly who can see what.

Built in the open

  • Open source. Every line of PassBundle is public on GitHub, under the GPL and AGPL, so anyone can check it.
  • Proven foundations. The web vault, the browser extension, and the server are built on two widely used open-source projects, credited at the foot of every page. We follow their security fixes every week and ship them quickly.
  • Our own apps, on one core. The desktop and phone apps are ours: one Rust library does the encryption, the vault format, and sync for all of them, so a fix lands everywhere at once. On phones, the key for fingerprint or face unlock stays in the phone's own secure hardware.
  • Encryption changes are slow on purpose. Any change to encryption, keys, or sign-in needs a written design and test vectors before it's merged.
  • Tested by machines. Every change runs through more than a hundred automated tests, from the server's API to real browsers with the real extension, on Windows, Mac, and Linux.
  • An independent audit of PassBundle's own changes is planned. We'll publish the report here.

Protecting your account

  • Two-step login with an authenticator app, a security key such as a YubiKey, or email codes, in every plan.
  • Password guessing is stopped per account. After 10 wrong tries, an account allows one attempt a minute, no matter where the guesses come from.
  • New devices are announced. When your account signs in on a new device, you get an email with when and from where.

For businesses and MSPs

  • MSP staff can't take over client accounts. Your provider's staff are never part of your account recovery, so they can't reset your people's master passwords.
  • Install commands are credentials. Each one is random and can be limited to your email domains or replaced at any time. Joins through it are recorded.
  • Everything an MSP does is logged, with who, when, from where, and in which client.
  • Directory tokens are scoped and shown once. An organization's Entra provisioning token reaches only that organization's people and groups, is stored hashed, and can be replaced or revoked at any time.
  • No remote control. PassBundle's Windows app takes no commands from the server, so a compromised server can't run anything on your computers. It installs only updates signed with our offline key.

Reporting a vulnerability

Please report security issues privately, never in a public issue:

  • On GitHub: open the repository's Security tab and choose Report a vulnerability, in the apps or the server.
  • By email: security@passbundle.com.

Tell us what you found, how to reproduce it, and what an attacker could do with it. We'll reply as soon as we can and keep you updated until it's fixed.

While you research

  • Give us reasonable time to fix an issue before you disclose it.
  • Only test against accounts and servers you own, or have explicit permission to test.
  • Don't access, change, or delete other people's data, and don't degrade the service for others.

We won't pursue legal action against research done in good faith under these rules.
